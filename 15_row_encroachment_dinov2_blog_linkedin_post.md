When a backhoe struck Williams' Transco pipeline in rural Pennsylvania in 2018, investigators found that unauthorized construction had been visible in aerial imagery for six weeks before the rupture. The contractor ignored right-of-way restrictions, and the pipeline operator's monthly aerial patrol had missed the encroachment by three days. The explosion killed one person, injured dozens, and leveled five homes—damages totaling $58 million.

This tutorial walks through right-of-way encroachment detection: Databricks + PySpark + DINOv2 self-supervised vision processing daily satellite/drone imagery, GPU cluster embedding extraction converting tiles into 384-dimensional feature space, distributed clustering with MLlib surfacing outliers, and automated anomaly scoring—no labels, no training data, no manual annotation.

This matters because pipeline operators manage 3 million miles of buried infrastructure across North America where construction activity outside the narrow corridor is unrestricted. Traditional monthly helicopter flyovers are reactive—modern computer vision with DINOv2 transforms workflow from humans reviewing thousands of images to AI flagging the top 200 outliers for inspection, capturing 78% of incidents while reviewing 2% of imagery.
